# Competitor Teardown

Use this when the user provides a video URL, screenshots, transcript, or asks to learn from a high-performing same-lane video.

## Teardown Checklist

Record:

- URL/source:
- Video length:
- Topic:
- First frame:
- First 3 seconds text:
- First 10 seconds plot promise:
- First visible number:
- Main contradiction:
- Protagonist identity:
- Trust shortcut:
- Disaster preview:
- Subtitle style:
- Color/style:
- BGM and sound effects:
- Cut frequency:
- Transition types, duration, and semantic purpose:
- Opening montage flashes and later callbacks:
- Tension peaks, valleys, false wins, and temporary reliefs:
- Exact numbers, dates, countdowns, and visible/hidden scoreboards:
- Comment triggers:
- CTA:

## Opening Analysis

Classify the opening:

- Number hook
- Disaster preview
- Familiar-person trap
- False bargain
- Reverse-identity hook
- Moral warning
- Cliffhanger

Then explain:

- Why the viewer keeps watching.
- Which emotion is activated: greed, fear, regret, anger, recognition, sympathy, justice.
- What information is intentionally withheld.
- Which opening images preview later consequences.
- When the opening stabilizes into the main story.

## Optional Transition Analysis

Use this only when transitions materially contribute to retention or when the user specifically asks about editing.

For each important transition, record:

- outgoing scene and incoming scene;
- approximate duration;
- hard cut, dissolve, double exposure, match cut, wipe, glitch, or other effect;
- semantic reason: time, place, causality, contrast, irreversible turn, or emotional aftermath;
- whether the next shot stabilizes or continues unnecessary motion.

Reject a transition insight if it is only “this effect looks cool.” Extract the causal reason it works.

## Optional Tension Analysis

Use this only when the story is long or its rises and falls are difficult to explain in concise prose. Do not require every sample to have a false win or temporary relief.

Map the story as a pressure wave:

`desire -> reward -> hidden cost -> relief -> worse baseline -> deadline -> collapse`

Identify the viewer question opened at each beat. Note callbacks, exact balances, payment dates, public/private contrasts, and the moment an apparent win changes meaning.

## Adaptation Rules

- Imitate: structural beat, pacing, hook mechanism, subtitle density, emotional contrast.
- Do not copy: exact story, exact phrasing, unique character design, creator name/watermark, proprietary music.
- Transform into our lane by changing industry, relationship, cost, protagonist, and lesson.
